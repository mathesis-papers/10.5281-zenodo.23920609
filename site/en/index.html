Refuting Peled et al.'s Section IV conjectures after Theorems 1 and 3, Eqs. (10) and (12): sharp fourth-order bounds without filter normal form

Haobo Ma, Wenlin Zhang

Paper

Can bounds used to recognize quantum entanglement survive removing a balancing condition on the subsystems? Haobo Ma and Wenlin Zhang answer no to two conjectured extensions: states without entanglement can exceed the proposed limits. They also establish an exact replacement bound for one family, rather than merely supplying counterexamples.

A separable state is a probabilistic mixture of product states, requiring only local preparations and shared randomness. Record joint expectations of complete local Hermitian measurement operators, orthonormal under the trace inner product, in a correlation matrix. Its square subdeterminants are called minors; collect the size-h minors into another matrix. For 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞, Schatten norms summarize its principal stretching factors, or singular values: the trace norm (p = 1) sums them, while the operator norm (p = ∞) takes the largest, not the largest entry. These quantities are independent of the orthonormal bases. Filter normal form requires both subsystems individually to be completely unbiased, or maximally mixed. Peled, Te’eni, Carmi and Cohen conjectured that two separability bounds remained valid without this requirement.

The first counterexample pairs three vectors of a two-level system, or qubit, (1,0), (1,2)/√5 and (1,−2)/√5, with three orthogonal labels in a four-level subsystem, with equal probabilities. This preparation is visibly separable. Add a proportion t of the completely mixed joint state. The authors calculate one third-order minor exceeding the conjectured operator-norm threshold, 3/(64√2), throughout 0 ≤ t ≤ 1/200. One oversized entry forces the norm above that threshold. Positive noise makes the state positive definite, so the failure is not confined to singular states. However, this construction does not refute the companion conjecture’s different third-order trace-norm threshold.

To address that companion, the authors pair four pure qubit states pointing toward a regular tetrahedron’s vertices with four orthogonal labels in a D-level subsystem. For 5 ≤ D ≤ 8, these separable states violate both fourth-order extensions for every Schatten parameter, even with completely mixed noise up to proportion 1/1000. Positive noise again gives positive definiteness. The qubit marginal is maximally mixed, but the larger marginal is not. The four correlation rows give just one fourth-order minor row; all its norms equal the product of the four correlation singular values.

The replacement theorem bounds this fourth-order quantity by 1/(192√3) for every separable qubit–D-level state with D ≥ 2. The noiseless tetrahedral state attains it for every D ≥ 4, establishing sharpness in that range. For the proof, rescale the three qubit measurement rows by √3, leaving the identity row unchanged. A local state’s purity is the trace of its square. Purity bounds make each rescaled qubit coefficient vector’s length at most √2 and its partner’s at most one; the triangle inequality then bounds the mixed matrix’s trace norm by √2. The arithmetic–geometric mean inequality bounds its four singular values’ product, maximized when they are equal. Undoing the rescaling gives the constant, which the tetrahedron attains.

This optimum also limits a design-based picture. The relevant pure-state designs are equally weighted families averaging to the maximally mixed state, with constant pairwise overlaps. Their overlap matrix has full rank; the four-dimensional qubit operator space therefore allows at most four members. A matching family spanning a D-level subsystem needs at least D. Thus no such common-size pair exists for D ≥ 5, although tetrahedral states with orthogonal labels still attain the unrestricted optimum. This does not refute the broader, less precisely characterized conjecture about “quantum designs.”

The authors also correct a missing square root in an earlier appendix. The sum of correlation singular values except the largest is bounded by the square root of the product of one minus each marginal’s purity. This uses the earlier separable covariance inequality of Zhang and collaborators, also presented by Gittsovich and collaborators. The 2×4 tetrahedral state saturates the correction and violates the printed version. In equal local dimension d, for d ≤ h ≤ d², the operator bound already follows from the realignment, or CCNR, condition that correlation singular values sum to at most one: it detects nothing beyond CCNR there. At h = d² this covers all Schatten parameters; Li, Poon and Sze previously established the corresponding trace-norm comparison.

The manuscript supplies written arguments for these conclusions. Its exact-arithmetic program checks selected examples and finite parameter ranges, not the unrestricted theorems. According to the authors, the linked Lean evidence refutes only the unrestricted third-order operator bound using the fixed 2×4 rational witness t = 1/1000; it does not prove the noise intervals, sharp replacement, or neighboring consequences. The significance is a precise distinction between balanced-state and unrestricted bounds, not a failure of correlation-based entanglement tests generally. The original filter-normal-form theorems remain intact. Other orders and dimensions, the broader design characterization, and the cited entropy and discord questions remain unsettled here.
